# Voice and Perspective Craft Review

## Objective
Preserve or develop voice from evidence already present in the manuscript, an author-provided voice guide, or explicit author approval. The legacy filename is retained for compatibility. This task does not infer origin and never injects a personal stance, experience, emotion, or quirk merely to appear human.

## Inputs
- Voice baseline and character/narrator profiles from **manuscript_analysis.md**.
- Revised manuscript after lexical, structural, and emotional passes.

## Steps

### 1. Optional Voice Audit

Use the following prompts only when voice development is an author-approved goal. They are `STYLE_HEURISTIC` questions with no pass count:

- [ ] Every sentence is roughly the same length and structure across a paragraph
- [ ] No opinions stated — only neutral reporting of events or facts
- [ ] Source-supported uncertainty or mixed feelings were flattened during revision
- [ ] First-person register already present in the source was flattened
- [ ] No humor, edge, irony, or personality — reads like a press release or Wikipedia article
- [ ] Intentional fragments, asides, or unusual rhythm were over-smoothed

Document relevant passages without scoring them as evidence of authorship.

### 2. Source-Supported Viewpoint

Identify moments where an existing viewpoint was flattened or could be clarified from supplied author guidance. The following are examples only and must not be inserted unless the source or author establishes the stance:
- An explicit opinion: *"I genuinely don't know how to feel about this."*
- A contradiction held openly: *"It was impressive and also kind of repellent."*
- A refusal to resolve: *"The truth was probably somewhere boring in the middle — but I kept thinking about it."*

Never assign an opinion to the author, narrator, or character. When support is missing, ask a question or provide a bracketed proposal for approval.

### 3. Rhythm and Intentional Texture

Preserve purposeful fragments, long sentences, pivots, and trailing thoughts. Suggest new rhythm only when it serves meaning or approved voice. There is no required short/long sentence quota, and irregularity is not an origin signal.

### 4. Acknowledged Complexity
Preserve ambivalence already supported by the source. A single clear emotion is valid. New ambivalence, second-guessing, or unwanted feelings are author decisions, not automatic edits.

- **Before (supported but flat):** *She was relieved it was over, and ashamed of that relief.*
- **After (same supported feelings):** *She was relieved, which made her feel worse about being relieved.*

See also **emotional_intensity_analysis.md** for embodiment — this step is about emotional complexity, not just physicality.

### 5. First-Person Signals (Where Authorized)

For first-person or close-third narrators, preserve interiority already established in the source. The following examples illustrate forms an author may choose; they are not default insertions:
- *"I keep coming back to..."* / *"What I didn't expect was..."*
- *"Here's what I didn't say:"* / *"Looking back, that was the wrong thing to fixate on."*
- An admission of ignorance: *"I had no idea what she meant. I still don't."*

For third-person limited, preserve established free indirect discourse. Do not create thoughts or experience absent from the source.

### 6. Character-Specific Craft Review
Using provisional voice profiles from `manuscript_analysis.md`, document source-supported differences among major voices. Do not infer personality from stereotypes. Where useful, record:
- Their default rhetorical register (clipped / expansive / ironic / earnest / detached)
- How an already supported viewpoint is expressed (blunt contradiction / reluctant admission / dry aside / open bewilderment)
- What "productive mess" looks like for them (trailing sentences / mid-thought corrections / abrupt topic-drops)

Apply only approved proposals consistent with each profile. Similarity across voices may be intentional and is not origin evidence.

## Deliverables
- Optional voice audit: relevant passages and the source evidence for each observation.
- Proposed or approved revisions, clearly labeled.
- Per proposal: source support or author-approval status.
- Character-specific craft notes for each selected voice, with no minimum count.
- Open voice questions requiring author review.

## Acceptance Criteria
- Enabled voice findings are reviewed or intentionally retained.
- Accepted changes are source-supported or explicitly author-approved.
- No passage is made artificially quirky and no new fact, experience, feeling, opinion, or stance is invented.
- Cross-check against `character_voice_analysis.md`: changes reinforce, rather than contradict, established voice evidence.
